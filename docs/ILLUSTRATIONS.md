# Illustrations brief

The visual bar is the cover artwork (brand bible, page 1): dense, layered, hand drawn blueprint and copper on textured paper. Every illustration must look like the same hand on the same sheet.

The clicks follow the chapter grouping proposed in STORYBOARD.md (27 September). Clicks reveal regions of these illustrations with masks and wipes. SVG is kept only for lines that must move: the dimension line, the section cuts, the Act 1 column and the Congresso.

## Production rules for every illustration

1. **Size:** 3840 × 2160 (16:9), PNG. The whole frame is the drawing; there's no type in the image.
2. **Two inks on paper, and only two.** Copper (burned copper #762F0B with warmer copper light #D89C67 for glow) is the idea: loose, hand drawn, expressive. Blueprint (#5F92B8 with pale blue #8DB5D2 washes) is reality: precise, technical, measured. Paper is warm parchment (#F3DDC0) with a subtle matte grain. Ink is used sparingly for anchors.
3. **Keep copper and blue apart in space.** Where they touch, they meet at a clear seam, as on the cover. That lets each illustration be separated cleanly by colour into a copper layer, a blueprint layer and a paper plate, and each layer can then be revealed on its own.
4. **Fixed places that must stay clear in every illustration** (so the composition never moves, and the type and figure always have their place):
   * **The figure's spot:** a clear area around 48% across, 75% to 88% down (about x 1780 to 1920, y 1620 to 1900 at 3840). The man is placed there separately; never draw a person there.
   * **The title block:** bottom right, from 71% across and 72% down (x 2730 to 3650, y 1560 to 2060). Keep it paper.
   * **Top band:** the top 8% stays light. The serif statements sit there or in the middle band.
5. **No people** in any illustration, except as tiny dots where specified. The figure is one drawing, reused.
6. **Brasília is original geometry.** A long straight axis crossed by curved wings is the gesture. It must never be a tracing or a lookalike of Lúcio Costa's pilot plan (no airplane or bird outline, no recognisable Eixo Monumental layout). No Niemeyer buildings: the Congresso is Edu's own drawing, added in SVG.
7. **Illustration, never evidence.** No numbers, words, logos, charts or real places in the images. Every fact on screen is typed by the deck.
8. **Deliver each illustration as a pair** where a click turns copper into blue: the same composition, once as the copper sketch (idea) and once as the blueprint drawing (reality). If the generator can't hold the composition across two images, deliver one image with both inks, separated in space as in rule 3.

## The figure

**F. The man from the cover, high resolution.** Same pose, proportions and clothes as the cover (seen from behind, arms at his sides, grey jacket, light trousers, dark shoes, short shadow at his feet). Drawn at about 1600 px tall on plain paper, so it can be cut out cleanly. In the cover artwork he's only about 95 px tall, too small to cut out for a 4K stage.

### 3. Brasília from the air (Acts 2 and 9, the mirror)
A planned capital seen from above, in copper: the long straight axis, the curved residential wings of dense blocks, a lake with an organic shore, roads, fine trees. It occupies the upper third, centred. At the four edges of the sheet, small loose clusters (satellite towns). The lower two thirds are open paper down to the figure's spot.
* Click 3, **Brasília**: reveal along the axis first, then the wings outwards.
* Click 4, **Scale**: no new image. SVG footpath and notations.
* Click 5, **Built by**: the satellite towns at the edges reveal.
* Click 26, **Worth doing**: the same image returns (the mirror).

### 4. The governance masterplan and its section (Act 3)
One sheet, two drawings. Upper third: a perfectly symmetrical copper masterplan of four large rectangular blocks around a formal central plaza (a grid of square cells), ceremonial and slightly too perfect. Lower third: the same thing in blueprint section at human scale, a long low wall on the left, a wide empty gap in the middle (the figure's spot), a narrow tall wall on the right. ### 5. Target line and network (Act 4)

### 6. The structure under the number (Act 5)
A section drawing: a long copper roof beam in the upper middle band (the number sits on it, typed by the deck), resting on six piles going down into the ground. The two piles on the left are clean and even; the four on the right are each drawn in a different hand and line weight, slightly mismatched. The ground in section below with fine hatching.

* Click 12, **Underneath**: the roof, then the piles top to bottom. The SVG property line.

Thousands of tiny hand drawn pages (portrait rectangles, some with a folded corner), scattered across the middle band. Dense clusters where many near identical pages pile up (duplicates). Most pages in warm pale copper, a minority (about one in five) in crisp blueprint.
* Click 14, **Batch**: nothing yet. SVG fan of meanings.

Right half: a hill in fine contour lines. On top, a gleaming copper skyline, slightly too perfect, with a thin warm glow. ### 9. The plant and its twin (Act 8)
Two tall process columns in elevation, standing on the ground line either side of the figure's spot. Left: the real plant in dense copper, with trays, nozzles, pipes, a few warm glowing points. ## Real material (unchanged, never generated)

## One risk to check early

Dense fine linework can turn to mush on a washed out projector. Each illustration will go through the projector simulation before it's approved. If one fails, the fix is heavier lines and fewer of them, not more contrast.
