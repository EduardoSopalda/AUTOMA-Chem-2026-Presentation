# From Brasília to the Plant Floor
## Speaking script, lean storytelling version (script of record from 28 September 2026)

Eduardo Sopalda. AUTOMA Chem 2026, Berlin, 26 October. Opening panel contribution.

Target rehearsal time: 8 to 8½ minutes for a 10 minute live slot. Source: `AUTOMA-Chem-2026-Script-8MIN.pdf`, transcribed word for word below.

### What changed after Chandar's feedback

* Fewer disclaimers: one authentic admission remains early; repeated qualifications were removed.
* No digital twin definition: the script goes straight to the trust question for an automation audience.
* "Humans do. Always." is the final line: nothing follows it.

### 1. THE ARCHITECT

I am an architect. Not as a metaphor. I studied architecture, I drew buildings, and then I spent the last twenty years designing something less visible: data and AI governance.

I work inside a chemical company, like many of you, and I have got plenty of these things wrong myself. But I keep seeing the same problem: we design from above, not always for the person who has to live with the result.

---

### 2. BRASÍLIA

Let me take you to Brasília. From the air, it is extraordinary: monumental, precise, unforgettable. But at street level, it can feel very different. The scale is vast. The distances are large. A city can make perfect sense from a helicopter and feel completely different when you walk through it.

We build governance in a similar way. We have policies, controls, committees, operating models—and, occasionally, a beautiful RACI that makes everyone feel civilisation has finally arrived.

For about ten minutes. Then someone asks who is actually accountable.

Then the framework reaches the plant.

I have been the person with the beautiful drawing. I have presented the framework, watched people nod, and then watched them carry on exactly as before. They were not being difficult. I had built them a plaza nobody wanted to stand in.

---

### 3. WHEN AMBITION BECOMES EVIDENCE

That matters when we talk about sustainable digital transformation. We set ambitions, targets and roadmaps. We invest in systems, automation and AI. But eventually, ambition has to become evidence.

A carbon number looks like chemistry. It is a data product.

Behind one apparently simple number sit supplier declarations, energy data, production records, transport data, allocation rules and assumptions. Much of it comes from systems that were never designed to agree with one another.

So the challenge is not simply calculating the number. The challenge is being able to explain it.

Where did it come from? Who owns it? What definition sits behind it? And who is prepared to stand behind it when someone asks a difficult question?

You cannot report what you cannot trace.

---

### 4. AI SCALES WHAT IS THERE

Then, inevitably, someone says: "Why do we not just use AI?"

I am optimistic about AI. But AI does not remove ambiguity. It can scale it.

If five people use the word "batch" to mean five different things, putting an AI agent on top does not make the organisation clearer. It just gives the confusion a faster processor.

Sometimes AI is the answer. Sometimes the answer is better data. Sometimes it is a better process. And occasionally it is simply agreeing on what a word means before we ask a machine to interpret it.

---

### 5. DIGITAL TWINS AND TRUST

And take digital twins. I do not need to define them for this audience. The interesting question is whether anyone believes them.

If equipment data is incomplete, if yield means something different in two systems, or if nobody is accountable for the assumptions, the twin is still only a representation on a screen.

It becomes valuable when the operator trusts it, the engineer understands what sits behind it, and someone is accountable when it is wrong.

---

### 6. CLOSE

So I want to leave you with one question.

What is one word in your organisation that means something different depending on which floor you are standing on?

Ask it at lunch. You may discover that the answer tells you more about your governance than another hundred pages of framework ever could.

Agents do not own risk. They do not carry accountability.

Humans do. Always.
