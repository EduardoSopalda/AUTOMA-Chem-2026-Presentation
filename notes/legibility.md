# Back row legibility

Edu's rule: anything meant to be read must be readable from the back row of a hall for 250 people, while staying quiet.

## Assumptions (to replace with the real room)

* Screen image about 6 m wide. Each of the 1920 stage pixels is then about 3.1 mm.
* Back row about 22 to 24 m from the screen.
* For comfortable reading, a lower case letter's x-height should be at least 1/350 of the viewing distance. At 24 m, that's about 69 mm, or 22 stage pixels.

## What that means on the 1920 by 1080 sheet

| Tier | Use | Minimum size |
|---|---|---|
| **Read** | Anything the audience must read: statements, labels, the question, scale notations, credits that replace spoken words | Source Serif 4 at **48 px**, DM Sans at **44 px** |
| **Texture** | Things that feel like a real drawing but don't need to be read from the back: the title block, source lines, drawing notes | Any size, but never carrying meaning the talk depends on |

The first draft broke this in several places. The cover's question was 21 px, readable only to about the middle of the hall. Labels in the placeholders were 22 px. Every "read" item is now at least 44 px.

The source lines for the Act 5 figures stay small in the title block. That's standard practice for a source. The spoken script carries the claim, and the web version shows the source at reading size.

**To confirm at the venue:** screen width and the distance to the back row. If the screen is smaller than 6 m, the minimums go up.
